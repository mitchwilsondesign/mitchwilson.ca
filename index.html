Explore the Intersections

Select the following topics to explore how information design and instructional design overlap.

  1. Clarity and Comprehension

    Both information design and instructional design strive to make information clear and understandable. Information design focuses on organizing and presenting information effectively, while instructional design aims to create learning experiences that help learners understand and remember the information.

  2. Visual Communication

    Information design often relies on visual elements such as charts, graphs, and diagrams to convey complex information quickly and effectively. Similarly, instructional design uses visual aids and multimedia elements to enhance learning experiences and make abstract concepts more concrete.

  3. Hierarchy and Structure

    Information design emphasizes the importance of organizing information hierarchically and structuring it logically to help users navigate and understand content. Instructional design employs similar principles to organize learning materials and activities in a way that scaffolds learning and builds upon previous knowledge.

  4. User-Centered Design

    Both disciplines prioritize audience needs and preferences. Information design creates user-friendly interfaces tailored to the target audience, while instructional design considers learners' backgrounds, preferences, and learning styles for designing materials and activities.

  5. Simplification and Reduction of Cognitive Load

    Information design simplifies complex information and reduces cognitive load through digestible chunks and visual cues. Instructional design also minimizes cognitive load by breaking down learning materials into manageable units and offering scaffolding and support for learners.

  6. Feedback and Iteration

    Both fields prioritize feedback and iterative design. Information designers refine designs through user feedback, while instructional designers gather feedback from learners and instructors to enhance instructional materials and improve learning outcomes.

  7. Accessibility and Inclusivity

    Both information design and instructional design aim for content accessibility to diverse audiences, including those with disabilities and from various cultural backgrounds. This involves considering factors like readability, language use, and providing alternative formats to ensure effective access and engagement for all users.

Inclusive Design Ties It All Together

My Master of Design in Inclusive Design at OCAD University (2023–2026) connects each step: structuring information and learning so people with different abilities and needs can use it. The thread started in my undergraduate work, including the capstone and the Universal Design for Learning prototype, and led to my research on digital accessibility for learners with autism, presented at ISIRC in 2025.
